## Initial Insight

When ecommerce merchants expand across multiple channels to grow their top line, they unknowingly create a compounding operational and commercial gap. Customer interactions fragment across platforms, financial performance becomes opaque, and management relies on manual coordination — including spreadsheets, which merchants use precisely because of their flexibility and zero-friction setup. The insight is that multichannel expansion is both inevitable and underserved: merchants need the operational orchestration and the commercial clarity to sustain it, without losing the control and adaptability they already have. LATAM merchants, where digital commerce maturity is still developing, feel the operational layer first — but the commercial blind spot is always underneath it.

---

## Problem (Outcome → Motivation → Gap)

**Outcome:** Run a profitable, multichannel ecommerce business that generates real yield without requiring constant personal orchestration.

**Motivation:** Financial independence through leverage. Ecommerce was chosen because technology was *supposed* to deliver the dream: automated sales, synced inventory, margins managed from anywhere. Instead, merchants got a second job — 23-hour shifts updating tools, recalculating prices per channel, adapting to policy changes, and manually handling every customer interaction. The promise of the internet business was freedom. What they got was a more fragmented, less profitable version of the corner store they were trying to avoid. Despacho Bonsai exists to give them back the dream they were sold but never received.

**Gaps:**

- Customer interactions fragment across every channel with no unified way to handle them — every new channel adds a new inbox, a new tab, a new place to burn hours
- Pricing and margin decisions are made on instinct because calculating them per channel is too costly in time — creating invisible margin leaks merchants don't see until it's too late
- Financial performance across channels is opaque: no true net view, no signal on what's actually working
- Channel management overhead eventually exceeds the marginal revenue of the channel — so merchants *shrink* their selling surface to survive, the opposite of their goal
- Off-the-shelf tools are fragmented, custom tech is out of reach — merchants cobble together manual workflows and spreadsheets not because they want to, but because nothing connects it all

## Value Proposition

**Tagline:** *Sell everywhere. Manage from one place. Get your life back.*

**Sub-benefits:**

1. **Your best sales and support rep, on every channel, always on.** AI agents handle pre-sale questions, post-purchase support, order lookups, upsells, and returns — automatically pulling real store data, building carts, and closing conversations — so no message goes unanswered and no sale slips through.
2. **See your real numbers, not your channel numbers.** One view of true net profitability across all channels. Know what's working before your next decision, not after. Export-ready data that plugs directly into the spreadsheets you already use.
3. **One rule, every channel.** Set your pricing logic and shipping rules once. Despacho Bonsai adapts them automatically to each channel's conditions — no more recalculating every promotion manually.
4. **One brand, everywhere you sell.** Consistent experience whether customers find you on Shopify, MercadoLibre, Amazon, or WhatsApp — without you being the connective tissue.
5. **Scale the operation, not the headcount.** Add channels and volume without adding people, meetings, or manual processes.

> **Positioning note:** Despacho Bonsai does not replace spreadsheets. It facilitates and enhances them — providing clean exports, pre-built templates, and the data infrastructure that makes spreadsheet management faster and more accurate.

**Short-term win:** Despacho Bonsai replaces a fragmented five-tool stack — CS helpdesk + shipping aggregator + per-channel analytics dashboards + financial spreadsheets + manual WhatsApp ops — with one platform built natively for LATAM multichannel merchants at SMB pricing.

**Long-term moat — two compounding powers:**

1. **Switching Costs** — every channel configuration, agent personality, pricing rule, customer history, and financial benchmark built inside Bonsai makes migration progressively irrational. The moat deepens every month a merchant stays.
2. **Counter-Positioning** — global incumbents (Gorgias, Linnworks) cannot serve LATAM SMBs without gutting their pricing model and rebuilding their marketplace integrations. They won't. That window is ours to own, and it closes as the market matures.
3. **Data Network Effects (horizon)** — as the merchant base grows, Bonsai accumulates LATAM-specific benchmarks: channel fee comparisons, shipping rate optimization, conversion patterns by vertical. That data becomes a moat no new entrant can replicate.

## Growth Strategy & Business Model

**Short-term traction — first 30 merchants:**

Intelligence-led outreach: Claude polls live public data (Shopify storefronts, MercadoLibre listings, social channels, response time tests) and structures it into a personalized ecommerce operations audit for each target merchant. This audit is delivered via WhatsApp/Instagram DM for SMB founders and LinkedIn for ecommerce managers at larger brands. It is not a sales pitch — it is the first product experience. A two-page diagnostic that makes merchants think *"how did they know all this?"* before a single demo is scheduled.

Parallel agency track: Outreach to 5–10 target agencies and ecommerce consultants, approached as **implementation partners** — not just referrers. Offer: co-branded onboarding experience, partner certification (raises their credibility with clients), and ongoing revenue share.

> **Note on the public audit tool:** Keep the full audit manual and proprietary for now. The personalization and curation IS the competitive advantage of this outreach. A self-serve public version loses the "how did they know?" effect and teaches competitors the methodology. Once the first 50 outreach cycles are complete, a simplified *teaser* version can be considered as a landing page lead magnet.
> 

**Long-term growth loop:**

- Agency partner network as primary distribution engine — revenue share creates self-sustaining referrals
- Merchant word-of-mouth amplified by fast, visible ROI (target: time-to-first-win under 14 days)
- LATAM ecommerce operator community — newsletter, WhatsApp group, events — positioning Bonsai as the knowledge hub for multichannel merchants in Mexico and beyond

**Agency revenue share model:**

| Model | Structure | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One-time flat fee | $300/merchant | Creates no retention alignment |
| % of Year 1 MRR | 20% | Partial alignment |
| ✅ **Ongoing % of MRR** | **15–20% forever** | **Agency earns passive income only while merchant stays → perfect churn alignment** |

**Business equation:**

- **Revenue:** Subscription tiered by number of channels or GMV range — target $200–600 USD/month SMB tier. Optional usage component on agent conversations.
- **Agency rev share:** 15–20% of MRR ongoing
- **Key levers:** Trial conversion rate · Time-to-first-win (target: <14 days) · Monthly churn (existential above 5%) · Cost per agent conversation (variable, tied to Anthropic API)
- **Unit economics:** At $400/month ACV and 3% monthly churn → LTV ≈ $13,300. CAC must stay under $2,500. Agency channel clears this comfortably.

## PMF Narrative — Full Synthesis

The insight behind Despacho Bonsai is not that merchants are using the wrong tools, but that the tools available were never built for the reality of operating across multiple channels simultaneously, particularly in a market like Mexico where digital commerce is growing faster than the infrastructure to support it. When a merchant opens a second channel — a MercadoLibre store alongside their Shopify, or a WhatsApp line alongside their Amazon listings — they don't just double their revenue surface. They double their operational weight: more inboxes, more pricing calculations, more shipping decisions, more data living in disconnected places. The merchants who feel this most acutely are not failing merchants. They're growing ones. And the spreadsheets they rely on to hold it all together are not the problem — they're the proof that merchants are smart enough to build their own systems when nothing adequate exists.

Instead, they got a second job. Customer interactions fragment across every channel with no unified way to respond without burning hours or burning out. Pricing and margin decisions are made on instinct because calculating them per channel is too time-consuming, creating invisible margin leaks that compound quietly over months. The promise of the internet business was freedom. What they got was a more fragmented, less profitable version of the corner store they were trying to avoid. Despacho Bonsai exists to give them back what they were sold but never received.
